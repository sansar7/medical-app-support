Dental Morphology Support

Updated October 10, 2026. Dental Morphology is a prototype educational 3D tooth atlas for dental students. It is not on the App Store yet; this page describes builds that contain the October 2026 changes.

Email: sansargupta10@gmail.com

Supported platforms: iPhone and iPad with iOS/iPadOS 26 or newer, and Macs with Apple silicon (M-series) running macOS 26 or newer. Intel Macs are not supported.

For a problem report, include the app version and build, device model, operating system version, the tooth or specimen you were viewing, and the steps that led to the issue. A screenshot can help. Do not send patient information.

Exploring the atlas

The app opens on a complete permanent dentition. Tap or click a tooth, or use Choose tooth, to study it. All teeth returns to the full arch, and the reset button restores the camera. On iPhone and iPad, drag to rotate and pinch to zoom. On a Mac, drag to rotate and pinch or scroll to zoom.

Each numbered position shows separately segmented enamel, dentin and pulp from a micro-CT specimen. Use Enamel, Dentin and Pulp to show or hide each tissue without moving the model; hide enamel and dentin to inspect the pulp chamber and canals. Additional scan specimens at the end of a tooth's details opens four separate micro-CT demonstrations. Two of them offer Endo, which isolates that specimen's own pulp.

On a Mac, and on an iPad with a hardware keyboard, the Atlas menu lists every tooth and specimen. Escape returns to all teeth, Command-0 resets the camera, and Shift-Command-R opens Sources & credits.

What the models and colors represent

Each model is an individual anatomical example, not a standard for every patient. Some positions are mirrored counterparts or class examples where the source does not document the original side; the app labels these. Segmentation into enamel, dentin and pulp was automatic and has not been reviewed by an anatomy educator or endodontist, so fine apical anatomy and some fissure detail may be incomplete.

Enamel and dentin colors and translucency are an illustrative rendering. The shading uses each specimen's measured enamel thickness and published optical properties of enamel and dentin, but micro-CT records structure, not the tooth's real color. Enamel is shown slightly whiter than dentin to make the tissues easy to tell apart.

Anatomy summaries and canal-configuration percentages come from cited textbooks and studies. Percentages describe study populations, not a particular patient. Sources & credits lists every reference and model source.

Accessibility

Every tooth can be chosen from a list, without using the 3D view. VoiceOver reads tooth names and layer states. The app follows Reduce Motion, Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast and Dynamic Type.

Troubleshooting

  • "3D model unavailable": choose another tooth or reopen the app. If it persists, reinstall the app and send a report.
  • The model looks too small or off-center after rotating or resizing: press the reset button.
  • A tooth takes a moment to appear: detailed specimens are large and load in the background the first time they are opened.
